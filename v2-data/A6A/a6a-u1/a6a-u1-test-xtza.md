> First, they make a budget (预算). They write down how much money they get from pocket money, doing chores, and their lemonade stand (柠檬水售卖摊) near their home. This clear plan helps them know how much they can use each week.
>
> Second, they learn about needs and wants. Needs are things they must have, like lemonade supplies, ice and cups. Wants are fun things they don't really need, such as candy and comic books. They stop spending too much on wants.
>
> Third, they use simple ways to manage money. They look for low prices and things on sale to save more. They set small goals and save little by little. They also give some money to help people and animals in need.
>
> At last, they divide their money into three parts: spend, save, and give. They stick to (坚持) their budget and become good at managing money wisely.

# Megan's Wonderful Trip

> Megan liked collecting small things. But she often spent her pocket money on snacks and toys right away.
>
> One day, she found her friend Kyle had more money. But they got the same pocket money every week.
>
> "Why do you have more money than me?" Megan asked.
>
> Kyle smiled and said, "I save my money in the bank, not just in a piggy bank(存钱罐)at home."
>
> Megan felt surprised. She asked her mom for help. Her mom told her, "Saving a little bit over time is better than spending all your money quickly."
>
> Megan decided to stop buying things she didn't really need. She put money into her piggy bank first. Then, with her parents' help, she opened a kids' bank account(账户). Her parents also helped her make a simple plan:
>
> * Save a little money every week
> * Give a little to help people in need
> * Keep a little to spend on things she wants
>
> Not long after that, Megan saved enough money for a family trip. She visited new places, learned many new things, and had a really wonderful time. On this trip, she learned that saving money in a smart way helps her stop wasting money and makes her dream trip come true.

# Present tenses (I am doing / I do) for the future[cite: 1]

## A Present continuous (I am doing) with a future meaning[cite: 1]

[*LAYOUT: Left column contains explanatory text, right column contains a visual calendar illustration.*][cite: 1]

This is Ben's diary for next week.[cite: 1]
He is playing tennis on Monday afternoon.[cite: 1] He is going to the dentist on Tuesday morning.[cite: 1]
He is meeting Kate on Friday.[cite: 1]

[*VISUAL: A desk calendar titled "CALENDAR" with three entries: "Monday Tennis 12:00 pm", "Tuesday Dentist 10:30 am", and "Friday Kate 6:00 pm".*][cite: 1]

In all these examples, Ben has already decided and arranged to do these things.[cite: 1]

**I'm doing** something (tomorrow etc.) = I have already decided and arranged to do it:[cite: 1]

* **A:** What are you doing on Saturday evening? (*not* What do you do)[cite: 1]
  **B:** I'm going to the cinema. (*not* I go)[cite: 1]
* **A:** What time is Katherine arriving tomorrow?[cite: 1]
  **B:** Half past ten. We're meeting her at the station.[cite: 1]
* I'm not working tomorrow, so we can go out somewhere.[cite: 1]
* Steve isn't playing football next Saturday.[cite: 1] He's hurt his leg.[cite: 1]

We do not normally use **will** to talk about what we have arranged to do:[cite: 1]

* What are you doing tonight? (*not* What will you do)[cite: 1]
* Alex is getting married next month. (*not* will get)[cite: 1]

We also use the present continuous for an action just before you start to do it.[cite: 1] This happens especially with verbs of movement (**go/come/leave** etc.):[cite: 1]

* I'm tired. I'm going to bed now.[cite: 1] Goodnight.[cite: 1] (*not* I go to bed now)[cite: 1]
* '**Tina**, are you ready yet?' '**Yes**, I'm coming.' (*not* I come)[cite: 1]

## B Present simple (I do) with a future meaning[cite: 1]

We use the present simple when we talk about timetables and programmes (for example, transport or cinema times):[cite: 1]

* I have to go.[cite: 1] My train leaves at 11.30.[cite: 1]
* What time does the film start tonight?[cite: 1]
* The meeting is at nine o'clock tomorrow.[cite: 1]

You can use the present simple to talk about people if their plans are fixed like a timetable:[cite: 1]

* I start my new job on Monday.[cite: 1]
* What time do you finish work tomorrow?[cite: 1]

But the continuous is more usual for other personal arrangements:[cite: 1]

* What time are you meeting Kate tomorrow? (*not* do you meet)[cite: 1]

**Compare:**[cite: 1]

| Present continuous | Present simple |
| :--- | :--- |
| What time are you arriving?[cite: 1] | What time does the train arrive?[cite: 1] |
| I'm going to the cinema this evening.[cite: 1] | The film starts at 8.15.[cite: 1] |

When you talk about appointments, lessons, exams etc., you can use **I have** or **I've got**:[cite: 1]

* I have an exam next week. *or* I've got an exam next week.[cite: 1]

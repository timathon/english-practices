## Unit 6: Past continuous (I was doing)

### Section A

Study this example situation:

[*VISUAL: Illustration showing two people playing tennis on a court, with a digital clock displaying 10:30 in the foreground.*]

Yesterday Karen and Joe played tennis. They started at 10 o'clock and finished at 11.30.
So, at 10.30 they **were playing** tennis.

they **were playing** =
they were in the middle of playing, they had not finished

**was/were + -ing** is the past continuous:

| Subject | Auxiliary Verb | Main Verb (-ing) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| he/she/it | **was** | **playing** |
| we/you/they | **were** | **doing**<br>**working** etc. |

---

### Section B

**I was doing** something = I was in the middle of doing it at a certain time. The action or situation started before this time, but had not finished:

[*LAYOUT: Timeline showing "I started doing" -> "I was doing" -> "I finished doing" along a time axis marked with "past" and "now".*]

- This time last year I **was living** in Hong Kong.
- What **were** you **doing** at 10 o'clock last night?
- I waved to Helen, but she **wasn't looking**.

---

### Section C

Compare **I was doing** (*past continuous*) and **I did** (*past simple*):

**I was doing** (= in the middle of an action)
- We were **walking** home when I met Dan. (in the middle of walking home)
- Kate **was watching** TV when we arrived.

**I did** (= complete action)
- We **walked** home after the party last night. (= all the way, completely)
- Kate **watched** TV a lot when she was ill last year.

---

### Section D

You can say that something **happened** (past simple) in the middle of something else (past continuous):
- Matt **phoned** while we **were having** dinner.
- It **was raining** when I **got** up.
- I **saw** you in the park yesterday. You **were sitting** on the grass and **reading** a book.
- I **hurt** my back while I **was working** in the garden.

But we use the past simple to say that one thing happened *after* another:
- I **was walking** along the road when I **saw** Dan. So I **stopped**, and we **talked** for a while.

Compare:
- When Karen arrived, we **were having** dinner. (= we had already started before she arrived)
- When Karen arrived, we **had** dinner. (= Karen arrived, and then we had dinner)

---

### Section E

Some verbs (for example, **know** and **want**) are not normally used in continuous forms (**is + -ing**, **was + -ing** etc.). See Unit 4A for a list of these verbs.
- We were good friends. We **knew** each other well. (*not* we were knowing)
- I was enjoying the party, but Chris **wanted** to go home. (*not* was wanting)
